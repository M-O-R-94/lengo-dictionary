# Lengo dictionary packs

Offline learner's-dictionary and learning-content packs for the Lengo iOS app:
16 languages, words placed in twelve learning bands (A1.1 … C2.2). The app downloads
them from this repository's releases. This repository holds data only.

## Learning content (`lengo-content-*`)

Each language also has a learning-content pack: everyday phrases and sentence patterns
from the Wikivoyage phrasebooks, short dialogues and reply pairs Lengo arranged from
those lines, idioms, slang, collocations, confusing pairs and word families from
Wiktionary, and example sentences from Tatoeba (native speakers only, each with its
number and author). Nothing is written or translated by Lengo; every item records its
source, licence, reference and review status (1 source text, 2 chosen or grouped
automatically, 3 arranged by Lengo; none reviewed by native speakers). A phrase's level
is estimated from its hardest dictionary word. The app ships the everyday situations of
all sixteen languages built in.

## What the levels mean

- The .1 and .2 steps split each CEFR level for practice. **They are Lengo's own
  steps, not official CEFR levels.**
- English (CEFR-J Wordlist, Octanove Vocabulary Profile) and Swedish (Kelly list) hold
  exactly the words of those published CEFR-labelled lists, with their levels. For the
  other fourteen languages the band is **an estimate from word frequency** (wordfreq);
  each entry records which applies (`band_source` 9 = estimated).
- Meanings share their word's band: no open source gives levels per meaning.

## What a pack contains

One read-only SQLite database per language (`PRAGMA user_version` = schema version),
compressed with xz. `manifest.json` lists every pack's size and SHA-256 (compressed and
unpacked), entry and band counts.

- `entry`: headword, part of speech, band, band source, frequency rank, gender, reading
  (pinyin, kana, vowelled Arabic, stressed Russian, romanised Hindi and Korean), IPA.
- `sense`: up to six short English meanings; `example`: Wiktionary usage examples
  with English translations (quotations from books and songs are left out). A word
  without one gets the shortest fitting Tatoeba sentence by a native speaker that
  uses the word, with its English translation, as its first meaning's example;
  `example.source` names it ("Tatoeba #… by …"), and is empty for Wiktionary's.
- `native_sense`, `native_example` (German, Spanish, French, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Dutch, Polish, Portuguese, Russian and Turkish): definitions and example
  sentences from the word's own Wiktionary, without translations. `native_sense.source`
  is empty for these; a later release may add definitions written by AI and checked by
  a second AI review, with `source` "AI (…)" and marked in the app.
- `term`: search keys for headwords, inflected forms and readings.
- `concept`, `entry_concept`: translations. A concept is one sense of an English word
  that has a Wiktionary translation table; two entries in different packs that link to
  the same concept translate each other. Each word is stored once, in its own pack.

## Known gaps

- Bands outside English and Swedish are frequency estimates, and frequencies are a 2021
  snapshot. English and Swedish cover only their lists' words (about 9,000 and 8,000). Hindi, Korean and Turkish frequency lists are shorter, so their highest
  bands are thinner.
- Translations exist only where Wiktionary's translation tables list a word: about
  half of Chinese, Japanese and Korean entries have one, more for European languages.
- Meanings are in English. Definitions in the word's own language exist for eleven
  languages (not Arabic, Chinese, Hindi or Swedish), where that language's Wiktionary has
  the word; Chinese is left out for now (traditional characters, raw markup).
- Example sentences: Korean (27% of entries) and Hindi (30%) have far fewer than the
  other languages, because Tatoeba has few sentences by native speakers with an English
  translation in them. A Tatoeba example is matched to a word automatically and shown
  under its first meaning, which isn't always the meaning the sentence uses.
- Lemmas are chosen automatically from frequency lists; some rare homographs and
  dialect words remain. Report problems in this repository's issues.
